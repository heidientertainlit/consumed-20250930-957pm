---
name: Taste group presentation
description: Product rule for presenting Tribe clusters to users without manufactured identities.
---

Present Tribe clusters as unnamed groups beneath the original Tribes section heading. Lead with an emotional “percentage — feeling” line and a short italic sentence that explains the human connection, supported by recognizable media covers, simple media types, and member previews. Keep generated names and abstract evidence labels out of the user-facing identity. Only the highest-scoring mixed cross-media group uses the “A lot in common” framing. Other groups use grounded category feelings such as “Your book people,” “Same comfort zone,” or “Same rabbit holes,” based on actual dominant media mix or strongest genre evidence, including YouTube and podcasts.

**Why:** Generated labels can misrepresent a mixed taste cluster and use language that does not mean much to a typical user. Emotional positioning makes the connection inviting, while concrete media and people keep it understandable and credible.

**How to apply:** Backend names and stable IDs may remain for clustering and routing, but cards and detail views describe the feeling and concrete connection rather than expose a generated name. Use the actual fit percentage; never invent separate category percentages.
