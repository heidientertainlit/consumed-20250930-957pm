---
name: Rated feed card structure
description: Approved hierarchy for rated and reviewed posts in the main feed.
---

Keep the poster, title, stars, match score, short review, and reviewer identity together as the primary media moment. A rating and its review text may arrive as separate rating-only and thought records; pair same-user/same-media companions created close together so stars stay with the visible review, and suppress the redundant rating-only slide. Additional ratings and comments belong together beneath that primary media moment, in one white discussion section with avatars, short names, optional stars, text, and reaction hearts.

**Why:** The user approved separating the primary review from discussion, then provided examples and explicitly asked for additional ratings and comments in the same place. Rendering companion records independently made review cards appear to lose their stars.

**How to apply:** Preserve both thumbs, Reply (including its bubble icon), Rate, Share, post reporting, comment reporting, and threaded replies when changing rated feed cards. Standalone rated/list-activity cards must use the same grouped thumbs-up/thumbs-down treatment as the main card path rather than the legacy heart footer. Never collapse those capabilities into decorative or nonfunctional controls. Reply previews must be readable in guest mode without exposing private profile fields. Once the comment request succeeds, the actual flattened comment tree is authoritative over denormalized counts: show a real reply preview when present, and hide stale reply counts when no comment row exists.

If the viewer has rated the title, put their own rating first in the shared discussion section, labeled “You,” even when there are no comments. Do not leave it beside the primary poster or below other replies.

**Why:** The user explicitly said, “But put my own rating at the top of the comments if I've rated it.”

**How to apply:** Use the actual saved rating, including half-stars, and preserve distinct comments rather than hiding every message from someone who also rated the title. Only identical review/comment text may be combined.

The featured “What People Are Saying” heading belongs inside its carousel's white card, matching the font of Trending now but slightly smaller. Omit the “Real ratings and reviews from your feed” subtitle.

**Why:** After trying serif styles, the user explicitly requested the same font as Trending now.

**How to apply:** Match Trending now's sans-serif heading family, weight, leading, spacing, and color, preserving the smaller size. Keep this heading specific to the featured Now review carousel.

For all Now rating and review cards, put avatar and shortened reviewer name first, then commentary at its original size, then the smaller standard serif title and season. Commentary may be clamped. Shorten multi-surname display names using the first surname's initial, even for the owner or accepted friends.

**Why:** The user approved this hierarchy, then asked for additional rating cards to match it. Only the first carousel needs “What People Are Saying” to orient people entering the app.

**How to apply:** Share the presentation across all Now rating and review cards, independently of whether the introductory heading is shown. Preserve stars, match pills, related ratings, all reaction controls, and owner delete.

Use the standard serif font, not Fraunces, for media titles on these rating/review cards.

**Why:** After trying the bundled Fraunces font, the user explicitly asked to switch these cards' media titles to the serif font instead and repeated that the title still looked like Fraunces.

**How to apply:** Use an explicit Georgia / Times family rather than the browser-dependent generic UI serif. Keep this scoped to the shared Now rating/review title styling. Preserve its size, weight, placement, and the separate sans-serif discovery heading; do not change fonts elsewhere.

Use a subtle overlapping-card edge plus a simple upper-right “>” to suggest more reviews, without dots, “Next” labels, thumbnails, or a previous/next navigation strip. Keep the heading slightly smaller than Trending now.

**Why:** The user rejected the added next-review strip and preview, then specifically requested an upper-right “>” and a heading slightly smaller than Trending now.

**How to apply:** Preserve swipe navigation and show the overlap only when the carousel contains multiple posts. The corner chevron advances to another review and must not overlap reporting. Do not introduce another labeled navigation strip.
